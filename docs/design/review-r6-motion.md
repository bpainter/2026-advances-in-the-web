# Design review: Still in motion demos (F1 opener, M1 to M5)

Reviewed 28 October 2026 against `docs/design/still-in-motion-demos.md`, `docs/design/review-r6.md`, `docs/design/review-v5.md`, `docs/design/demo-glass-spec.md` and `docs/design/demo-wrappers-spec.md`.

Evidence. Screenshots are 1920 by 1080 captures of the running dev server through `/present?s=<id>`, stepped with ArrowRight. `rm/` means `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-bermonpainter-Sites-web-unleashed-talk-2026/6b2d6583-dfb5-4ace-8751-a1f9a529508e/scratchpad/r6/review-motion/`. Files are `<page>--s<n>.png`, where `s0` is step 1.

- `rm/*.png` come from Playwright's Chromium 145 with `--enable-blink-features=CSSGridLanesLayout`, 3.5 s per step (5 s for M5).
- `rm/chrome/*.png` reshoot M2 now and M3, M4 and M5 in the installed Google Chrome 156 with the same switch. Chromium 145 has no `CustomElementRegistry` constructor, so M3 now shows its iframe fallback there (see M11). Judge M3 from `rm/chrome/`.
- `rm/burst/` and `rm/vid/` hold frame bursts and video frames of the motion steps (M4 s3, M5 s1). `strip-*.png` are tiled frames at 5 to 10 fps.
- Probes: `rm/probes-*.json` (spotlight rects and radii, text under 14 px), `rm/m3probe.mjs` (iframe and app heights), `rm/anim.mjs` (time to ring), `rm/m5ring.mjs` (ring state over time).

Not checked: WebKit and Firefox, the projector, axe, `?glass=off`, reduced motion, intro stills and posters (not captured yet).

## Summary

| Severity | Count |
|---|---|
| High | 4 |
| Medium | 11 |
| Low | 10 |

The five sections read as real product UI. The M3 editor and the M4 help center match the core demos' polish, and the M1, M2 and M5 photos are believable. The worst problems sit on the steps that carry each demo's proof: M2's start state is a layout no browser ships, M2's rings run off the card, M4's key step is hard to see, and M5's used-to page draws a second blue box.

## What passes

- **Product realism.** The M3 editor (tabs, SKU, Draft chip, sales channels, v3 badges and CAD chips) and the M4 help center (breadcrumb, numbered step cards, return window card) match D1 to D6. The M1 lookbook and the M5 catalog read as a real store.
- **Same UI two ways.** By eye, M3 s1, M4 s0 and M5 s0 match between builds apart from the shown block (not diffed pixel by pixel). The other differences are listed in M1, H3, L1, L5, L6 and L9.
- **Features render.** Grid Lanes is on in both Chromium 145 and Chrome 156 with the switch (`display: grid-lanes` computed at s1). Scoped registries work in Chrome 156: the app grows in place and "Add tier" stays visible. The cross-document transition plays inside the frame in Chrome 156: the photo morphs and the name slides (`rm/burst/strip-m5-now-s1.png`, bottom row).
- **Code panel.** 24 px, no sideways scroll, and the highlights match the spec's code focus on every step checked. The M5 now panel shows the matching `view-transition-name` lines on s2 and s3.
- **Spotlight radii.** Card targets (radius 12) get ring corners of 19. Pins are concentric circles. The M5 hero (radius 10) gets 17. F1's rule bar is a clean pill. Rings appear 0.6 to 2.4 s after the click, including pointer moves and page loads, and M4 s3 at 3.3 s because of its busy loop. I did not run `pnpm walk`, so the 2000 ms forced-spotlight gate is unverified.
- **Copy.** No em or en dashes in any M1 to M5 or F1 label, bullet, intro, caution or wrapper string. No on-screen sentence runs over 15 words (citations excluded). Labels are sentence case.
- **F1 opener.** Five tiles in demo order, each with a use and a wait signal, and a two-step reveal. The rule bar ring sits inside the frame.

## Notes

- In one capture, `m5-now` s1 showed no ring at 5 s while three browsers were driving the server. Three solo reruns showed it by 2.5 s every time (`rm/m5ring.mjs`). I treat it as load.
- An earlier `m1-used-to` s2 capture at 3.5 s also had no ring. With 6 s between steps it is always there (`rm/m1-used-to--s2-long.png`), and `anim.mjs` times it at about 1.7 s, which includes the pointer move.
- I did not test plan B for M2 (`showSafari`) or the reduced-motion paths.
